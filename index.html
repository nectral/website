The problem

AI already runs in your browsers. Security can't see it, and blocking it backfires.

⇪

Data leaks through prompts

Source code, credentials, customer records and confidential files get pasted and uploaded into AI tools, many of which retain or train on inputs.

◌

Shadow AI is everywhere

Thousands of AI apps exist, new ones launch weekly, and AI is embedded inside approved SaaS. Nobody has an inventory.

⌁

Agents act on your behalf

AI browsers, sidebars and extensions read whole pages. Hidden prompt-injection text can steer them into leaking data.

⊘

Blocking pushes AI underground

Blanket bans send employees to personal devices and phones, where security has no visibility at all.

Network DLP and CASB see domains, not prompts. Endpoint DLP struggles with chat-style web apps. Most AI security tools inspect your prompts in their cloud. Enterprise browsers mean replacing the browser.

How it works

One extension. One console. Four steps from blind spot to policy.

  1. 01

    Discover

    Force-install via your existing Chrome or Edge management. Within a day you see which AI apps, AI extensions and agents are in use, by whom and by department, each scored for data retention, training use and hosting region.

  2. 02

    Inspect on-device

    Typed prompts, pastes, drag-and-drop and file uploads are checked in the browser for secrets, PII including national IDs, payment cards, source code, and your own keywords and patterns.

  3. 03

    Redact, coach or redirect

    Choose the lightest action that works: allow, coach, warn with justification, redact, block, or redirect to your sanctioned AI tool. Reversible redaction keeps the answer useful.

  4. 04

    Govern and prove it

    Dashboards for usage, risk and coverage. Versioned policies with an audit-only simulation mode. High-severity events flow to Splunk, Sentinel or any syslog/CEF SIEM.

FAQ

Questions security teams ask first

Does Veil see or store our employees' prompts?

Not by default. Inspection happens in the browser. Events sent to the console contain rule IDs, counts and salted hashes rather than raw values. Evidence capture is an explicit, opt-in setting, encrypted and viewable only by a restricted role.

Do we have to replace our browser?

No. Veil is a Manifest V3 extension for Chrome and Edge, force-installed and configured through the browser management you already use. Firefox and Safari support is planned.

How does reversible redaction keep answers useful?

Sensitive values are replaced with consistent placeholders such as [PERSON_1] before the prompt is sent. The AI reasons over the placeholders, and Veil swaps the real values back into the response on the device, so the employee reads a normal answer.

What happens if the console is unreachable?

The extension keeps enforcing its cached, signed policy and queues events locally until it reconnects. It fails safe, not open.

Can we run it with no internet access at all?

Yes. The air-gapped mode runs fully on-prem with an offline AI app catalog, signed release bundles and an offline license file.

What about desktop AI apps and phones?

Veil starts in the browser, where most AI use happens. Desktop, IDE and API coverage are on the roadmap, and Veil is designed to sit alongside your SWG and endpoint DLP rather than replace them.
